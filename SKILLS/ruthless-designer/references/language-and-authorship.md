# Language And Authorship

Produces or revises product copy, a brief, a critique, a proposal, a report, or a design handoff. Governs language around the interface and labels inside it.

## Job

Clear, specific, human language fit for audience, format, product voice. Preserve meaning and distinctive voice. Smallest effective edit. Observable patterns as evidence. Never guess whether a person or a model wrote the text.

## Read Before Writing

- Read the full draft, source, and evidence before editing. Identify audience, format, desired action, product context.
- Preserve vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, humor, uncertainty, useful digressions, and polish when they carry voice. Leave strong sentences alone.
- Keep the writer's or product's meaning. Do not add claims, examples, statistics, quotes, sources, customers, metrics, or opinions. Name the source or mark the gap.
- Keep existing structure unless it harms comprehension or the task; explain a meaningful reordering in the change log. Preserve information architecture, routes, analytics, legal or SEO copy, accessibility text, and known product terminology unless the task changes them.

## Editing Rules

- Lead with the point when setup adds no context, tension, story, or character. Front-load a conclusion only when it improves clarity; do not force every section into point-detail-background.
- Active voice, direct verbs. Human subject where possible; replace abstract verb phrases with the actual action. Prefer names, numbers, dates, mechanisms, examples, user consequences over abstract importance.
- Keep substance, nuance, technical precision. Cut jargon, abstractions, tangled structure only when they block comprehension. Protect specific facts and useful detail; never smooth them into a broad importance claim.
- Every sentence must carry a fact, decision, user consequence, state, or next action. Keep useful uncertainty, self-awareness, humor, blunt language, spoken rhythm. Cut empty qualifiers and throat-clearing.
- Untangle hard sentences without flattening clear fragments, long spoken lines, pace changes, or distinctive cadence. Choose prose, lists, labels, headings from the content. Sentence case after a colon unless grammar, a proper noun, a title, or code requires otherwise.
- Em dashes rarely. Short copy usually needs none; longer copy gets one or two only when they beat a period, comma, or parenthesis.

## Patterns To Cut

Review: name the pattern, quote the literal text, smallest fix.

- **Binary contrasts:** state the conclusion; skip the opposing-claim frame.
- **Throat-clearing openers:** cut setup that delays the point.
- **Faux-insight setups:** cut lone-expert claims.
- **Colon reveals:** colons for lists, labels, quotes — not dramatic reveals.
- **Superficial analysis:** replace trailing `-ing` significance with mechanism or user consequence.
- **Importance puffery:** state the fact; reader judges importance.
- **Weasel attribution:** name the source or mark unverified. Never invent consensus.
- **Fake-strong verbs:** prefer `is`, `has`, or the direct verb when a grand verb hides the work.
- **Synonym cycling:** repeat the clear term; do not rotate names for the same actor, object, or action.
- **Negative listings:** state the positive definition or action once.
- **Dramatic fragments:** complete sentences unless a fragment has a clear product, interface, or voice purpose.
- **Robotic rhythm:** vary sentence shape when it helps; cut stacked fragments and repeated templates.
- **Rhetorical setups:** cut self-answered questions, baited reveals, theatrical prompts. Make the claim.
- **Fake-profound kickers:** end on the clearest concrete sentence or next action; no metaphor swap.
- **Summary endings:** cut recap; end on a takeaway or next action.
- **Formatting slop:** no emoji in headings, decorative mid-sentence bold, bullets where prose is clearer, headings over tiny sections.

## Default No-Go Language

Avoid unless product vocabulary, a quotation, a technical requirement, or the writer's real voice earns them:

- Inflated words: `delve`, `foster`, `leverage`, `utilize`, `facilitate`, `empower`, `streamline`, `robust`, `cutting-edge`, `paradigm shift`, `game changer`, `tapestry`, `realm`, `beacon`, `multifaceted`, `meticulous`, `intricate`, `paramount`, `transformative`, `elevate`, `embark`, `supercharge`, `harness`, `ever-evolving`.
- Empty adverbs: `just`, `literally`, `honestly`, `simply`, `actually`, `truly`, `fundamentally`, `importantly`, `crucially`, `inherently`, `inevitably`.
- Delaying phrases: `it's worth noting`, `it's important to note`, `at the end of the day`, `when it comes to`, `at its core`, `in today's world`, `in the age of`, `the reality is`, `the truth is`, `in terms of`, `with regard to`, `in order to`, `going forward`, `in this article`, `let's dive in`.

Pressure test: keep a term that carries meaning, uncertainty, emphasis, or domain precision.

## Design-Specific Application

- UI label names an action, object, state, or consequence — not marketing theater. Address the reader as `you` when product voice allows. Do not narrate “the user.” Omit unneeded gender; people-first language for disability; plain wording over culture-bound jokes and idioms.
- Permission purpose copy: one active sentence — what is collected and why. Reject “for a better experience.”
- Brief explains a decision, its product cause, and its proof target — not moodboard language hiding missing context. Critique uses `evidence -> damage -> cause -> fix`. No swagger, jokes, or vague verdict in place of inspection.
- Proposal marks content as real, synthetic, missing, or blocked. Placeholder copy is not product proof. Report uses literal subjects, direct findings, named sources, concrete next actions. Do not inflate ordinary changes into milestones or revolutions.

## Workflow And Output

1. Read the complete text. Internal note: core point and three to five voice signals.
2. Minimum effective edit. Preserve strong lines and structure unless they harm clarity or the task.
3. Check every claim against supplied evidence. Name an absent source instead of filling the gap.
4. Run the checklist below. Fix failures before returning the result.
5. Return the complete edited text and a short `What changed` section when editing was requested.

Detection or audit: report each found pattern with its quoted line and a short fix. Do not rewrite, score, or claim authorship. No text supplied: ask them to paste it. If audience, format, or goal changes the edit, ask one focused question.

## Gate

Pass only when:

- meaning, voice, product terminology, useful uncertainty intact;
- point early where that improves comprehension;
- claims use concrete facts or named sources, gaps marked plainly;
- direct verbs and active subjects carry the sentence;
- strong lines survived; edit matches the amount of actual slop;
- listed patterns, inflated language, formatting problems gone or justified;
- ending on a concrete point, takeaway, or next action;
- full output and short change log match the requested format.

# Language And Authorship

Use this route when the work produces or revises product copy, a brief, a critique, a proposal, a report, or a design handoff. It governs the language around the interface and the labels shown inside it.

## Job

Make language clear, specific, human, and fit for its audience, format, and product voice. Preserve meaning and distinctive voice. Make the smallest effective edit. Use observable patterns as evidence. Never guess whether a person or a model wrote the text.

## Read Before Writing

- Read the full draft, source, and evidence before editing. Identify the audience, format, desired reader action, and product context.
- Preserve vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, humor, uncertainty, useful digressions, and level of polish when they carry voice. Leave strong human sentences alone.
- Keep the writer's or product's meaning. Do not add claims, examples, statistics, quotes, sources, customers, metrics, or opinions. Name the source or mark the gap.
- Keep the existing structure unless it harms comprehension or the task. Explain a meaningful reordering in the change log.
- Preserve information architecture, routes, analytics, legal or SEO copy, accessibility text, and known product terminology unless the task changes them.

## Editing Rules

- Lead with the point when setup adds no context, tension, story, or character.
- Front-load a conclusion only when that improves clarity. Do not force every section into the same point-detail-background shape.
- Use active voice and direct verbs. Give actions a human subject where possible; replace abstract verb phrases with the actual action.
- Prefer names, numbers, dates, mechanisms, examples, and user consequences over abstract importance.
- Keep substance, nuance, and technical precision. Remove jargon, abstractions, and tangled structure only when they block comprehension.
- Protect specific facts and useful detail. Never smooth them into a broad claim about importance.
- Make every sentence earn its place by carrying a fact, decision, user consequence, state, or next action.
- Keep useful uncertainty, self-awareness, humor, blunt language, and spoken rhythm. Remove empty qualifiers and throat-clearing.
- Untangle sentences that are hard to follow without flattening clear fragments, long spoken lines, changes in pace, or other distinctive cadence.
- Choose prose, lists, labels, and headings from the content. Use sentence case after a colon unless grammar, a proper noun, a title, or code requires otherwise.
- Use em dashes rarely. Short copy usually needs none; longer copy gets one or two only when they clearly beat a period, comma, or parenthesis.

## Patterns To Cut

Name the pattern, quote the literal text, and state the smallest fix when reviewing copy.

- **Binary contrasts:** state the conclusion directly instead of framing it through an opposing claim.
- **Throat-clearing openers:** remove setup phrases that delay the point.
- **Faux-insight setups:** remove claims that flatter the writer as the person who sees what everyone else misses.
- **Colon reveals:** reserve colons for lists, labels, and quotes; write dramatic reveals as plain sentences.
- **Superficial analysis:** replace trailing `-ing` claims about significance with the mechanism or user consequence.
- **Importance puffery:** state the fact and let the reader judge its importance.
- **Weasel attribution:** name the source or mark the claim as unverified. Never invent consensus.
- **Fake-strong verbs:** prefer `is`, `has`, or the direct verb when a grand verb hides the work.
- **Synonym cycling:** repeat the clear term. Do not rotate names for the same actor, object, or action.
- **Negative listings:** state the positive definition or action once.
- **Dramatic fragments:** use complete sentences unless a fragment has a clear product, interface, or voice purpose.
- **Robotic rhythm:** vary sentence shape and paragraph length when it helps the point; remove stacked fragments and repeated templates.
- **Rhetorical setups:** cut self-answered questions, baited reveals, and theatrical prompts. Make the claim.
- **Fake-profound kickers:** end on the clearest concrete sentence or next action. Do not replace a weak metaphor with a stronger one.
- **Summary endings:** remove recap paragraphs when the reader has just seen the point. End on a concrete takeaway or next action.
- **Formatting slop:** remove emoji from headings, decorative mid-sentence bold, bullets where short prose is clearer, and headings for tiny sections.

## Default No-Go Language

Avoid these words and phrases in authored copy unless the product vocabulary, a quotation, a technical requirement, or the writer's real voice earns them:

- Inflated words: `delve`, `foster`, `leverage`, `utilize`, `facilitate`, `empower`, `streamline`, `robust`, `cutting-edge`, `paradigm shift`, `game changer`, `tapestry`, `realm`, `beacon`, `multifaceted`, `meticulous`, `intricate`, `paramount`, `transformative`, `elevate`, `embark`, `supercharge`, `harness`, `ever-evolving`.
- Empty adverbs: `just`, `literally`, `honestly`, `simply`, `actually`, `truly`, `fundamentally`, `importantly`, `crucially`, `inherently`, `inevitably`.
- Delaying phrases: `it's worth noting`, `it's important to note`, `at the end of the day`, `when it comes to`, `at its core`, `in today's world`, `in the age of`, `the reality is`, `the truth is`, `in terms of`, `with regard to`, `in order to`, `going forward`, `in this article`, `let's dive in`.

Treat this list as a pressure test. Keep a term when it carries meaning, uncertainty, emphasis, or domain precision.

## Design-Specific Application

- A UI label names an action, object, state, or consequence. It does not perform marketing theater.
- Address the reader as `you` when the product voice allows it. Do not narrate “the user.” Omit unneeded gender, use people-first language about disability, and prefer plain wording over culture-bound jokes and idioms.
- Permission purpose copy is one active sentence: what is collected and why. Reject “for a better experience.”
- A brief explains a decision, its product cause, and its proof target. It does not hide missing context behind moodboard language.
- A critique uses `evidence -> damage -> cause -> fix`. It does not substitute swagger, jokes, or a vague verdict for inspection.
- A proposal marks content as real, synthetic, missing, or blocked. It does not turn placeholder copy into product proof.
- A report uses literal subjects, direct findings, named sources, and concrete next actions. It does not inflate ordinary changes into milestones or revolutions.

## Workflow And Output

1. Read the complete text and keep an internal note of its core point and three to five voice signals.
2. Make the minimum effective edit. Preserve strong lines and structure unless they harm clarity or task performance.
3. Check every claim against the supplied evidence. Name an absent source instead of filling the gap.
4. Run the checklist below. Fix failures before returning the result.
5. Return the complete edited text and a short `What changed` section when editing was requested.

For a detection or audit request, stop after reporting each found pattern with its quoted line and a short fix. Do not rewrite, score, or claim authorship. If the user has not supplied text, ask them to paste it; if audience, format, or goal changes the edit, ask one focused question.

## Gate

Pass only when:

- the meaning, voice, product terminology, and useful uncertainty remain intact;
- the point appears early where that improves comprehension;
- claims use concrete facts or named sources, with gaps marked plainly;
- direct verbs and active subjects carry the sentence;
- strong lines survived and the edit matches the amount of actual slop;
- the listed patterns, inflated language, and formatting problems are gone or justified;
- the ending stops on a concrete point, takeaway, or next action;
- the full output and short change log match the requested format.

# Brand And Persuasion Surfaces

Use for landing pages, launches, portfolios, pricing, campaigns, services, commerce storytelling, and cultural/editorial public surfaces that must persuade, express identity, or reward discovery.

## Solve The Argument First

Identify:

- Offer: what is sold, launched, explained, or requested.
- Audience: who evaluates it and under what pressure.
- Intended response: signup, contact, purchase, upgrade, demo, download, subscribe, explore, remember, attend, or trust.
- Proof: artifact, screenshot, output, case, metric, testimonial, customer, security, or example.
- Objections: price, complexity, migration, lock-in, risk, time-to-value, support, or trust.
- Constraints: brand assets, SEO, analytics, legal copy, forms, IA, available media.

Build structure from offer, proof, and objections.

## Diagnose Information And Content

Before styling, map audience questions in decision order: `what is this -> is it for me -> how does it work -> why believe it -> what does it cost/risk -> what next`. Inspect nav labels, titles, claim/proof adjacency, repeated ideas, missing objections, buried terms, and whether every important object has a real content home.

Cut duplication, merge synonymous sections, rewrite vague labels as domain actions, and put proof beside the claim it supports. If the page needs three paragraphs to explain its own navigation, content architecture is dead.

Mystery and delayed reveal are valid for cultural, editorial, entertainment, and discovery surfaces when anticipation is part of the value. Preserve orientation, access, skip/exit, reduced motion, and a direct path for users with a concrete task.

## First Viewport

Commercial, service, conversion-led surfaces must carry:

- specific offer or product/category signal;
- concise support line;
- one dominant action;
- one credibility signal;
- visible hint of next proof, product, or mechanism.

Cultural, editorial, entertainment, and discovery surfaces must carry identity, one orientation cue, an intentional entry into the artifact or story, and reachable wayfinding. Dominant CTA, support line, or credibility badge is optional when it would flatten earned mystery into funnel sludge.

Keep it legible on a small laptop and mobile. Kill the four-line display headline, tag soup, secondary-CTA shouting match, decorative metadata, and hero art that hides what the product does.

Prefer a real artifact, screenshot, object, place, output, person, material, or interaction. If no credible asset exists, state the gap. Nested divs are not product proof.

## Page Rhythm

Choose only the section jobs the intended response needs; palette, not mandatory funnel:

- Claim and immediate evidence.
- Product mechanism or workflow.
- Comparison: old/new, manual/automated, current/target, plan/plan.
- Outcome or case.
- Objection and trust response.
- Pricing or fit decision.
- Final action at highest conviction.
- Encounter, fragment, context, sequence, archive/programme, or return path for cultural and editorial discovery.

Do not force pricing, objections, testimonials, or repeated conversion onto a surface whose job is exploration, remembrance, attendance, or cultural encounter. It still needs orientation and access.

Use an artifact stage, proof wall, comparison canvas, editorial path, or product-led landing when it strengthens the argument. Avoid equal icon-heading-paragraph cards, alternating text/image bands with no rhythm change, and the same eyebrow on every section.

Proof before decoration. Convert where proof peaks.

## Pricing

Lock the model before drawing cards: per-seat, usage, tiered, flat, freemium, enterprise, or custom.

- Align plan names, price, period, limits, CTA, and comparable feature rows.
- Say who recommended plan is for and why.
- Expose overages, taxes, currencies, billing period, custom quote, and hidden limits.
- Keep risk reversal—trial, cancellation, migration, SLA, support, refund—near decision.
- Tabular numerals for changing prices.
- Tooltips or disclosure for complex limits; never hide material terms.

Organize by fit and consequence, not a wall of checkmarks.

## Dark-Pattern Veto

Never earn conversion through confusion or exhaustion. Kill prechecked consent, hidden fees or material limits, fake scarcity, misleading countdowns, confirmshaming, disguised ads, forced continuity, obstructed cancellation, visual demotion of the honest choice, and refusal loops that ask the same question until the user surrenders.

Make price, renewal, consequence, data use, and reversibility legible before commitment.

## Reference-Led Work

Use screenshots, URLs, videos, HTML exports, brand assets, and generated section comps when they reduce guessing. Capture the narrowest useful evidence:

- Screenshot/image: hierarchy, grid, type scale, palette roles, radius, shadow, media treatment, copy density, signature detail.
- URL/page: relevant viewport and section crops; do not trust blank full-page captures when lazy load, canvas, or scroll effects interfere.
- Video: inspect beats—purpose, layout, motion, interaction, timing, implementation risk.
- Inspiration set: few references with reason each, not compressed moodboard graveyard.

## Visual Voice

- Choose type from audience/offer axis, not fashion. Contrast on purpose; keep display copy below billboard.
- Derive palette from real brand artifact, scene, product material, or image. Name roles before values.
- One committed visual constraint and one signature move.
- Give media deliberate crop, edge, caption, and inspection affordance.
- Asymmetric section roles and varied rhythm when story requires them.
- Literal, domain-specific copy: object, action, audience, consequence.

Kill reflexive purple-blue gradients, beige/brass luxury, decorative glass, terminal cosplay, faux brutalism, default editorial monochrome, tiny uppercase kickers, fake coordinates/version strips, random serif italics, and generic "empower/streamline/supercharge" claims.

Guilty until product provides an alibi.

## Immersive Effects

Use canvas, WebGL, 3D, particles, physics, globes, or scroll storytelling only when the effect is a real artifact, mechanism, place, data object, or requested brand experience. Prefer the existing stack. Add a dependency only when static media or CSS cannot carry the idea.

Require foreground readability, reduced motion, mobile/static fallback, offscreen pause, device-pixel/density caps, cleanup/disposal, and runtime proof.

## Pass Gate

Pass only when:

- The page answers—or deliberately stages—what it is, who it is for, why to trust or enter it, and what happens next; concrete-task users retain reachable direct path.
- Real artifact, context, or proof appears when experience promises it, and each section has distinct persuasive or experiential role.
- First viewport survives small laptop and mobile.
- Signature move is visible, useful, and specific to offer, artifact, story, or intended response.
- Applicable SEO, analytics, legal, forms, rights, and accessibility constraints remain intact.
- References and assets are named, verified, and compared against result.
